	\section{Abstract}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Poly(A) tail measurement to distinguish transcribing vs. poly(A) transcripts.
		\item Splicing is much more delayed than previously tought, for at least 10 kb. 
		\item Splicing mostly apparent only in poly(A) transcripts $\to$ they think it's delayed mostly after 3' end formation. 
		\item Lots of $\text{m}^{6}$A on unspliced RNA $\to$ deposition of $\text{m}^{6}$A happens before splicing.
		\item $\text{m}^{6}$A supressed around exon boundaries $\to$ $\text{m}^{6}$A topology should be established before EJC deposition.
		\item 3' end cleavage occurs multiple kbs behind transcription and is coordinated with transcription termination. The 3' end formation is recursive in many genes.
		\item Overall, they think that cleavage, termination and $\text{m}^{6}$A deposition occur earlier than most of the splicing.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section{Intro}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Spliceosome assembly is known to be rapid. 
		\item Splicing kinetics: estimates vary across methods.
		\item Previously, it was tought that EJC prevents $\text{m}^{6}$A deposition at exon boundaries. Here, they argue that $\text{m}^{6}$A is placed before splicing (and thus, before EJC placement).
		\begin{itemize}
			\item $\text{m}^{6}$A methylation machinery is known to interact with Pol II $\to$ permitting co-transcriptional methylation.
		\end{itemize}
		
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section*{Poly(A) notes}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Poly(A) site: cannonical AAUAAA motif.
		\item When Pol II reaches it, it keeps transcribing.
		\item Protein complex cut RNA there; poly(A) polymerase (PAP) adds $\sim 200$ As.
		\item Transcription termination:
		\begin{itemize}
				\item Torpedo model: Exonuclease XRN2 starts chewing the 5' end of RNA still transcribed by Pol II $\to$ when it catches Pol II, it knocks it off DNA.
				\item Allosteric model: Pol II changes conformation at poly(A) site, becomes less stable and eventually falls of DNA.
		\end{itemize}
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section{Results}
	\subsection{Development of a multimodal direct pre-mRNA sequencing technique + dFORCE distinguishes endogenously polyadenylated pre-mRNA}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Need to distinguish whether pre-mRNA has a poly(A) tail:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e}: undergoing transcription.
			\item pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a}: polyadenylated pre-mRNA, undergoing post-transcriptional maturation.
		\end{itemize}
		\item They pulled Pol II with associated pre-mRNA via immunoprecipitation. The pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a} was still somehow pulled (even thought it was already cleaved - otherwise it would not have the poly(A) tail); probably was somehow attached via the cleavage complex, or some chromatin components.
		\item They got about 90\% of pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} and 10\% pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a}.  
		\item 95\% of pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} have unspliced introns, while this is only 58\% for  pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a}.
		\item Nanopore sequencing requires poly(A) tail $\to$ added artificially during library preparation. This \textit{in vitro} poly(A) tail is shorter (20-80nt), so they get bimodal distribution of tail lengths, and classify by cut-off.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Also, the reads classified as pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a} have the start of poly(A) highly enriched by known poly(A) sites.			
		\end{itemize}		
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{The landscape of pre-mRNA 3' polyadenylation}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item They detect 42k poly(A) sites (PAS) across 11.6k protein-coding genes.
		\item Detected PAS mostly agreed with annotated PAS, the \underline{AAUAAA} signal, and were also detected by total RNA-seq.
		\item About 18\% of PAS were in introns (mostly downstream from canonical sites).
		\item Recursive polyadenylation: reads classified as pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a}, spanning between two PAS (avg. distance 531 nt). Suggesting that pre-mRNA with poly(A) tail can be cleaved again (at earlier PAS) and also polyadenylated again there.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Single-molecule kinetics of cleavage and termination}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Use pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} extending beyond distal PAS. Classified as cleaved vs. uncleaved, based on their 5' end.
		\item They model:
		\begin{itemize}
			\item $C_{50}$: distance beyond PAS where 50\% transcripts are cleaved.
			\item $T_{50}$: distance beyond PAS where density of Pol II active site drop to 50\% of coverage downstream of PAS.			
		\end{itemize}
		\item They estimate $C_{50}$ to 2.1kb (lower bound, because of issues with max. read lenght).
		\item Estimate of $T_{50}$ of 2.3kb. 
		\item When both estimates were available, the median difference was about 95 nt.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\section*{Splicing notes}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item \textbf{Spliceosome}: consists of snRNA and proteins. Recognizes 5' splicing site (GU), 3' site (AG), and \textbf{branch point A} (about 20-50nt from the 3' site).
		\item \textbf{Step 1}: Adenosine at branching point attack the 5' site, cutting the exon and forming a covalent bond with the intron $\to$ forming a loop (\textbf{lariat}).
		\item \textbf{Step 2}: The 5' exon free end attack the 3' site, forming a covalent exon-exon bond, releasing the lariat out.
	\end{itemize}
	
	\subsection{Discrepant evidence for co-transcriptional splicing catalysis}
	\begin{itemize}
		\item Discrepancy in splicing: pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} ending in exons often show upstream introns as spliced, but pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} ending in introns does not.
		
	\end{itemize}

	\subsection{Catalytic intermediates inflate co-transcriptional splicing estimates}

	\begin{itemize}		
		\item Catalytic intermediates (CI*): ending exactly at 5' exon - after step 1 of splicing, but before step 2 is finished.
	\end{itemize}

\subsection{Elongating pre-mRNA is globally unspliced for thousands of nucleotides}
\begin{itemize}		
	\item To avoid contamination by CI*, they analyzed pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} ending in the last intron, and also after PAS (but before polyadenylation) $\to$ about $\sim 90\%$ of upstream introns unspliced. Median context of such analysis was 2-3 kb.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Splicing catalysis is deferred until after polyadenylation}
\begin{itemize}		
	\item Pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a} was somehow spliced (27\% fully, 33\% partially, 40\% not).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The quantitative landscape of pre-mRNA m6A}
\begin{itemize}		
	\item They basecalled $\text{m}^{6}$A, about 200k sites, mostly known (DR\underline{A}CH motif). Using both total RNA (long reads, but without immunoprecipitation) and pre-mRNA.
	\item On pre-mRNA, $\text{m}^{6}$A was $\sim 17 \times$ depleted on introns. Somewhat smooth transition around exon-intron boundaries.
	\item $\text{m}^{6}$A sites found in pre-mRNA almost always found also in total RNA.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Single-molecule timing of $\text{m}^{6}$A deposition}
\begin{itemize}		
	\item Compared $\text{m}^{6}$A levels across pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e}, pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a}, CI*, and total RNA. Pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{e} had $\sim 50 \%$ of total RNA level, but both pre-mRNA\textsuperscript{a} and CI* had almost $100\%$ of total RNA $\text{m}^{6}$A level $\to$ $\text{m}^{6}$A deposition starts co-transcriptionally  and is mostly finished before splicing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{dFORCE resolves the \textit{in vivo} sequence of mRNA synthesis}
\begin{itemize}		
	\item Most of the previous results were reproduced for mouse brains.
	\item Some $\text{m}^{6}$A sites were hypermethylated on spliced pre-mRNA compared to total mRNA $\to$ possible demethylation during neuronal mRNA metabolism.
\end{itemize}
